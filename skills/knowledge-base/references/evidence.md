# Evidence

What the method rests on, and how strong each support is. Keep this honest: where the evidence
is mixed, it says so.

## Remembering

- **Retrieval practice and spacing.** Rated the two most useful study techniques in a broad
  review; rereading and highlighting rated low. Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham
  (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1).
  https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/
- **Test-enhanced learning.** Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science* 17(3).
- **Interleaving.** Rohrer & Taylor (2007), *Instructional Science* 35. Rated "moderate" by
  Dunlosky et al.: promising, less tested outside mathematics.
- **Method of loci.** A medium effect on recall in a meta-analysis. Twomey & Kroneisen (2021).
  https://doi.org/10.1177/1747021821993457

## Understanding

- **Modes of engagement (ICAP).** Learning increases from passive to active to constructive to
  interactive. Chi & Wylie (2014), *Educational Psychologist* 49(4).
  https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2014.965823
- **Active learning in STEM courses.** A meta-analysis of 225 studies found better exam
  performance than with lecturing. Freeman et al. (2014), *PNAS* 111(23). A later review
  questions the internal validity of many of the included studies and warns against equating
  behavioural activity with cognitive activity: Martella et al. (2023), *Educational Psychology
  Review*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-023-09826-1 . This is why the method asks for
  thinking, not clicking.
- **Elaborative interrogation and self-explanation.** Rated "moderate" by Dunlosky et al.
- **Prediction before feedback.** Brod, Hasselhorn & Bunge (2018).
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2018.01.013
- **Concrete first, then abstract.** Fyfe, McNeil, Son & Goldstone (2014), *Educational
  Psychology Review* 26. Its generality across domains is disputed: Kokkonen & Schalk (2021).
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09581-7
- **Comparing cases.** Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn (2013), *Educational Psychologist* 48(2).
- **Concept maps.** Building or studying them helps; building helps more. Schroeder, Nesbit,
  Anguiano & Adesope (2018). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-017-9403-9
- **Multimedia principles** (coherence, signalling, segmenting, pre-training). Mayer (2009,
  2020), *Multimedia Learning*. https://my.chartered.college/?p=6958

## Interaction design

- **Guided interactive lessons.** Brilliant's own account of its lesson design: a question
  first, concrete visuals before symbols, one idea per step, immediate feedback, then retrieval,
  fading help, varied contexts, spacing and interleaving.
  https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/how-brilliant-teaches-math/
  (A company describing its product, not an independent study.)
- **Explorable explanations.** Victor (2011), https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/ ;
  Victor, "Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction", http://worrydream.com/LadderOfAbstraction/ ;
  Case, "How I make an explorable explanation",
  https://blog.ncase.me/how-i-make-an-explorable-explanation/ and "4 more design patterns",
  https://blog.ncase.me/explorable-explanations-4-more-design-patterns/ . Practitioner
  knowledge, not controlled studies.
- **Implicit scaffolding.** Podolefsky, Moore & Perkins (2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6544 ;
  Moore, Herzog & Perkins (2013), *Chemistry Education Research and Practice*,
  https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2013/rp/c3rp20157k ; PhET design principles,
  https://phet.colorado.edu/en/about
- **Feedback.** Hattie & Timperley (2007), *Review of Educational Research* 77(1).

## Questions

- **Item-writing guidelines.** Haladyna, Downing & Rodriguez (2002), *Applied Measurement in
  Education* 15(3): one idea per item, no tricks, plausible wrong options from typical errors.
- **Three options.** Rodriguez (2005), *Educational Measurement: Issues and Practice* 24(2):
  a meta-analysis concluding that three options are optimal in most settings.
- **Certainty-based marking** (stating how sure you are). Gardner-Medwin,
  https://www.ucl.ac.uk/lapt/lpcfhelp.htm
