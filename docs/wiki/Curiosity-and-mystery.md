# Curiosity and mystery

## The claim

What moves a learner forward is the wish to close a gap they can see: "there is something here
and I do not know what it is". In a game that feeling is called mystery. So mystery is not
decoration in Mnemopolis; it is the game's version of the hunger to understand.

## Where it comes from

The information-gap account of curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994) describes curiosity as what one
feels on noticing a gap between what one knows and what one wants to know. Two consequences
matter for design:

- **A gap needs an edge.** Nobody is curious about a subject they know nothing about. Some
  knowledge has to come first, and then the missing piece becomes visible. This is why the town
  shows a locked door with something glinting behind it, rather than hiding the door.
- **The gap must look closable.** Curiosity fades when the answer seems out of reach. Later work
  finds that people are more curious when the information seems retrievable (Recht & Yeung,
  2026). This is why every barrier says what would open it.

## How the game uses it

| In learning | In the game |
|---|---|
| A question before its answer | Each lab starts with a prediction; each house is announced by the one before |
| A surprising result | The lab is built around one surprise; the wrong prediction is the useful case |
| Knowing there is more | Upper floors, locked doors, a boulder on a lane, silhouettes in the discoveries page |
| A hint, not a list | Thoughts aloud, a cat with a lantern at the edge of the screen, a rhyme found line by line |

## The rule that follows

**The basic path is explicit; the extras are never listed.** A newcomer must always see the next
step of the main story (the tracker, the house numbers, what people say). Beyond it the game only
hints. A checklist of secrets would turn curiosity into bookkeeping.

## Limits

Curiosity directs attention; it does not teach. What the learner does once curious still has to
be well designed (see [Understanding before memory](Understanding-before-memory)). And mystery
must never stand between the learner and the course: nothing hidden is needed to finish.
