# Brief: Port Octave

- **Subject:** music theory for guitarists, from the vibrating string to the open chord, with the
  physics of frequencies, beats and ratios that explains why some notes agree.
- **Learner:** a guitarist who strums a few open chords and songs but does not know why they work.
  No notation, no physics and no theory assumed. Reads English; Italian readers get the Do Re Mi
  names once.
- **Goals.** At the end the learner can:
  1. predict how the pitch of a string changes when it is fretted, and find the octave at half its length;
  2. explain why two close notes beat, and why notes in a simple frequency ratio sound smooth together;
  3. name the notes along a string and count the distance between them in half steps;
  4. build a major scale from any note with the pattern of whole and half steps;
  5. build major and minor triads and find their notes inside an open chord shape.
  Extra goals, claimed on the ending card only by players who did the house: place the minor
  pentatonic box for any key (`pentatonic`); use the circle of fifths to tell which keys share
  notes (`fifths`); name the chords of a key (`keys`).
- **Typical mistakes:** "an octave is eight notes, so eight frets"; "there are seven notes";
  "frets are evenly spaced steps of pitch, adding the same hertz"; "a six-string chord is six
  different notes"; "major and minor are unrelated shapes"; "the box at fret 5 is the blues scale";
  "consonance is pure physics" (or the opposite, "pure taste"); "a guitar can be tuned perfectly
  by harmonics".
- **Size:** standard (8 concepts).
- **Care:** curated. This is the second showcase course of the template: more sources (a survey of
  fourteen syllabi, `syllabi.md`), every number cross-checked, experts with tempers, a lock in every
  core house, real synthesised sound in every lab.
- **Level:** introductory, with upstairs going to intermediate (harmonics, the fifth-fret rule,
  equal temperament against the pure third, tuning by beats, the relative minor, the five open
  shapes).
- **Core** (the main story): `string`, `beats`, `twelve`, `scale`, `triads`. Five houses with one
  ground-floor station each: the validator's model (6.5 minutes a station, 4.5 a house, 20 seconds
  a final question) gives 57.7 minutes, and a sixth house or a second station anywhere would take
  the core over the hour. That decided two things: intervals have no house of their own (the half
  and whole step are taught at the Twelve-Hour Tower, the third and the fifth at the Choir House,
  as JustinGuitar and Applied Guitar Theory do), and every second idea of a house went upstairs.
- **Extras:** `pentatonic` (the box every guitarist meets), `fifths` (the circle of fifths and
  keys), `keys` (the chords of a key, I IV V vi). All three come after the major scale in every
  outline surveyed; none is needed by a core house.
- **Language:** English. Note names are the letters C D E F G A B; the Italian Do Re Mi is named
  once, on the page of the Twelve-Hour Tower.
- **Material received:** none beyond the owner's request: a second demo game on music theory, the
  right level of detail, a part on frequencies and interference to understand chords and ratios
  physically, the cycle of notes, good audio, oriented to guitarists, a survey of how others teach
  it, and game-like mini-games (which of two notes is higher, with points).
- **Decisions taken without the user:**
  - slug `fretboard`, town *Port Octave*, a harbour (the story brings the ship);
  - standard tuning and A4 = 440 Hz throughout, equal temperament everywhere except where the
    lab says "pure";
  - no staff notation (as in every guitar source surveyed); the fretboard, sound and simple
    diagrams are the language, and "written an octave higher than it sounds" is named once;
  - consonance presented honestly: simple ratios make harmonics coincide and stop beating
    (physics, Helmholtz, Plomp and Levelt), and how much people like it depends on culture
    (McDermott et al. 2016);
  - CAGED and modes are named as next steps, not taught;
  - experts: Aldo the luthier (string), Ilse the piano tuner (beats), Sam the bluesman
    (pentatonic), Cora the choir master (triads); the tower, the ladder, the compass and the hall
    are institutions. Two trades were added to the engine for them (`luthier`, `tuner`).
